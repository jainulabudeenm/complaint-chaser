# India: rules and escalation

Checked from the primary text in Nov 2026. Before you quote a rule in an email, check it again
against the source if it is older than about a year.

## Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020

Source: the notified text, G.S.R. 462(E), 23 July 2020
([PDF on taxguru](https://taxguru.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Consumer-Protection-E-Commerce-Rules-2020.pdf)).
The PDF is compressed: save it and run `pdftotext`.

| Rule | What it says (key words) | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| 2(2) | The rules "shall apply to a e-commerce entity which is not established in India, but systematically offers goods or services to consumers in India." | A foreign company (a web host, an app) says Indian rules do not apply. |
| 4(2) | Show the legal name, the address of the headquarters, and contact details of customer care "as well as of grievance officer". | The site hides who they are. |
| 4(4) | Appoint a grievance officer and "display the name, contact details, and designation of such officer on its platform". | No Grievance Officer is shown. Ask for one. |
| 4(5) | The grievance officer "acknowledges the receipt of any consumer complaint within forty-eight hours and redresses the complaint within one month". | They stall. |
| 6(3) | A seller on a marketplace (Amazon, Flipkart) shall not "refuse to refund consideration, if paid", for goods that are "defective, deficient or spurious", not as advertised, or late. | The seller is on a marketplace. |
| 7(1)(f) | An inventory store must give "a ticket number for each complaint lodged". | They give no ticket number. |
| 7(4) | An inventory store *(it sells its own stock on its own site)* shall not "refuse to take back goods ... or refuse to refund consideration, if paid", if the goods are "defective, deficient spurious", not as advertised or agreed, or late. | A defective product and a "no refund" policy. The rule has no exception for any product type. |

A store's own policy cannot remove these duties. Say it plainly: "Your policy cannot override Rule 7(4)."

## Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Source: [Act text, NCDRC](https://ncdrc.nic.in/bare_acts/CPA2019.pdf).

- **Section 34(2)(d):** a complaint can be filed at the District Commission where the consumer lives. The user does not need to file in the company's city, even if their Terms name a city.
- The website builder complaint used 2 grounds: "deficiency in service" and "unfair trade practice".

## The escalation ladder

1. **The company's support**, in writing. Ask for a supervisor, the reason in writing, the Grievance Officer and a complaint number.
2. **National Consumer Helpline (NCH):** [consumerhelpline.gov.in](https://consumerhelpline.gov.in), or call 1915. Free. The user files: it needs their login and phone OTP. The helpline can pass the complaint to the company. In the website builder case, the company logged the grievance number on its ticket. Quote the grievance number in every later email.
3. **Formal notice** to the legal address in the company's Terms. The Act does not require it. It shows you are ready to file, and it gives a clean date for "they were told".
4. **District Consumer Commission** through [e-jagriti.gov.in](https://e-jagriti.gov.in). It replaced e-Daakhil from 1 January 2025 ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/india-news/after-e-daakhil-govt-to-roll-out-e-jagriti-to-streamline-case-filing-124112700697_1.html)). No fee when the amount paid is up to Rs. 5 lakh ([PIB, 2022](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1843926&reg=3&lang=2)). It needs an affidavit *(a sworn statement, stamped by a notary)*, each document as a PDF and the user's ID proof. The user files.

## Card refunds and bank fees

- Some Indian cards charge a DCC fee *(Dynamic Currency Conversion: a fee on a rupee payment to a merchant registered abroad)* plus 18% GST. Example: the Amazon Pay ICICI card charges 1.99% from 15 Jan 2026 ([ICICI terms, PDF](https://www.icici.bank.in/terms-condition/tnc-for-amazon-pay)).
- In the website builder case, the card charged this fee on the refund too. ICICI's DCC rule says nothing about refunds. Only its foreign-currency rule says that a refund is charged. Ask the bank to reverse the fee on a refund.
- This fee belongs to the bank, not the merchant. Do not ask the merchant for it.
- Do not open a chargeback for a payment the user agreed to.
